# Shreya's LinkedIn Style Guide

Based on 17 weeks of posts and performance data.

---

## Voice

**Conversational but specific.** Not corporate, not try-hard casual. Like explaining something interesting to a smart friend.

**Honest about failures.** The posts that resonate most include what went wrong, what surprised me, what I'm still figuring out.

**Personal stakes.** Why do I care about this? What's the emotional engine? Even technical posts need a human reason.

---

## Structure That Works

### The Hook
Start with a **specific moment** or **surprising outcome**, not a thesis statement.

**Good hooks from top posts:**
- "Last week, I needed a plumber. He called during a meeting..." (6,504 impressions)
- "I had my first design crit this week. I was nervous." 
- "When my sister told me hosting on Squarespace would cost $300/year..." (4,064 impressions)
- "If you'd asked me on Wednesday, I would've said week 4 was a complete failure."

**Bad hooks:**
- "Here's what I learned this week..."
- "I've been thinking a lot about..."
- "This week I built X. Here are my takeaways."

### The Middle
- **One main insight**, not a listicle of surface observations
- **Specific details** — tools, costs, time spent, what broke
- **The turn** — what surprised me, what I got wrong, what changed

### The Close
End with **momentum**, not wrap-up:
- "Week 17. Still learning."
- "More next weekend."
- "Stay tuned for more."

Don't over-explain or summarize what you just said.

## What Works (From Top Performers)

| Pattern | Example | Why It Works |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| **Relatable problem** | Phone tag with the plumber | Everyone's experienced this |
| **Specific numbers** | "$0.11 to embed 1,000 wines" | Credibility + useful |
| **Honest skepticism** | "I'm not 100% convinced by this idea" | Refreshing, not salesy |
| **Building for someone else** | Sister's portfolio, nonprofit email | Shows generosity |
| **Vulnerability** | "I was nervous" / "I felt called out" | Human connection |

## What to Avoid

### LinkedIn-speak
- "Here's the thing"
- "I've landed on a strong opinion"
- "Let me break this down"
- "This is a thread about..."

### AI-sounding prose
- Too many em-dashes in a row
- Overly balanced sentences
- Generic observations without specifics
- "In plain English:" (trust your reader)

### Structural traps
- Listicles without emotional core
- "3 things I learned" when you really learned 1 thing
- Announcing your structure before delivering it
- Too many exclamation points (one per post max)

## The Shareability Test

Before posting, ask:

1. **Does it articulate something people feel but can't explain?**
   - "AI makes it easy to build garbage fast" ✓
   - "I built something cool this week" ✗

2. **Does it introduce a useful mental model?**
   - "AI gets you 80%. Taste gets you the rest." ✓
   - "AI is really helpful" ✗

3. **Does sharing this make someone look smarter?**
   - Practical insight they can borrow ✓
   - Inspiration without substance ✗

## Format Notes

- **Length:** 150-300 words for regular posts, up to 400 for meaty ones
- **Images:** Screenshots of the thing you built, not stock graphics
- **Links:** Include them, but the post should stand alone
- **Hashtags:** Skip them or use sparingly
- **Emojis:** One or two max, at natural moments

## Templates

### The Build Post
```
[Relatable problem or surprising outcome]

[What I built to solve it]

[1-2 specific learnings with details]

[What's next / what I'm still figuring out]

Week X of building with AI.
```

### The Reflection Post
```
[Specific moment that sparked reflection]

[The insight — framed as a mental model]

[Evidence from my experience]

[Why it matters / who it's for]
```

### The Tool Comparison Post
```
[Context: what I was trying to do]

[Tool A vs Tool B — specific differences]

[When to use each]

[What I'm still learning]
```
